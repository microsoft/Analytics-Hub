A Health scorecards

Each card shows the selected window's total (or session-weighted average), the change vs the previous equal window, and a 6-week trend sparkline. Friction and bot metrics turn red when they rise (up is bad for those).

B Sessions — expected vs actual

The shaded band is the expected range (seasonal-naive baseline: same weekday last week, ± a robust 80% interval). Points that break out of the band are marked — red for a drop, green for a surge. This is what turns "a line went down" into "this specific day was abnormal."

E Bot vs human transparency

Bot traffic silently inflates sessions and skews page rankings. This shows the split every day and flags days where the bot share spiked — the exact pattern that produced the corrupt captures we just fixed.

F Friction watch

Dead clicks, rage clicks, quick-backs and script errors are leading indicators of UX breakage that never show up in session counts. Each is tracked as % of sessions with a 6-week sparkline.

G External discovery — with quality

Where real external traffic comes from, with a NEW badge when a referrer appears for the first time (an early sign of a mention or a scraper). Internal hub/GitHub navigation is excluded.

H Pipeline health data downtime

Before trusting any number above, is the data itself healthy? Freshness, gaps, coverage, corrupt captures caught, and dev-traffic leakage — so a broken export never gets mistaken for a real traffic drop.

GitHub view/clone counts only retain ~14 days upstream, so any window longer than that on the repo side is stitched from stored daily snapshots, not a live query.
