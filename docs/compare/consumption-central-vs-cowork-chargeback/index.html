Breadth vs. billing mechanics

Consumption Central vs. Cowork Chargeback

Both report on Copilot credit consumption and cost, and both are free Power BI templates. The difference is scope. Consumption Central covers four products in one view. Cowork Chargeback goes deep on one — and does the billing mechanics the other does not attempt.

TL;DR

Start with Consumption Central when the question is "what are we spending on Copilot, in total, and where is it going?" It pulls Cowork / Work IQ, Copilot Studio, GitHub Copilot and Azure AI Foundry into one model so you stop reconciling four portals by hand.

Reach for Cowork Chargeback when you have to actually bill someone. It prices the same Cowork consumption three ways — pay-as-you-go, prepaid, hybrid — allocates a prepaid pool across departments by a model you choose, and ships row-level security so a department head sees only their own line.

They do overlap — here is where

Both cover Cowork credit consumption, both forecast forward, and both express credits in money. If Cowork is the only Copilot product you pay for and you do not need per-department billing models, either one will answer your question and you do not need both.

🎬 Consumption Central — two-minute walkthrough

What Consumption Central answers

  • What is our total Copilot spend this billing period, across every product?
  • Which product is growing fastest, and where will we land by period end?
  • Which departments, cost centres or countries are driving the spend?
  • Are we about to breach a prepaid pack — and how much capacity is sitting unused?
  • What is GitHub Copilot costing us next to Copilot Studio and Foundry?

What Cowork Chargeback answers

  • What does each department owe for Cowork this month, in money?
  • Would pay-as-you-go, prepaid, or a hybrid be cheapest for us?
  • How should the prepaid pool be split — by usage, headcount, or budget?
  • Which users are over their allowance, and who needs their limit adjusted?
  • Can I give each department head a view of their own line only?

Why the split exists

Chargeback is a genuinely different job from cost visibility. Allocating a prepaid pool fairly across departments needs allocation models, per-user allowances, and row-level security so people see their own numbers and nobody else's. That machinery is specific to how you have chosen to bill, and it does not generalise across four products with four different commercial shapes.

Consumption Central deliberately does not attempt it. It reports what was consumed and what it cost, across everything, with a forecast. Once you know the total and need to turn Cowork specifically into departmental invoices, Cowork Chargeback is the tool built for that step.

Reach for Consumption Central when…

  • You pay for more than one Copilot product
  • Finance is asking for a single number
  • You need a forecast to end of billing period
  • You want to see it before committing — sample data ships with it
  • You are choosing where to set limits in the admin centre

Reach for Cowork Chargeback when…

  • You have to issue internal invoices for Cowork
  • You are deciding between prepaid and pay-as-you-go
  • You need to split a prepaid pool across departments
  • You manage per-user credit allowances
  • Department heads need their own filtered view

Most teams that pay for several products run both

Consumption Central for the whole picture, Cowork Chargeback when the Cowork bill has to be split.
